## The real ground, and the shadow

**The shadow probe** (`probe_sun_shadow.tscn`, twenty variants of a box under
the world's settings) found that sun shadows on the ground were fine all along
and that three artefacts had stacked up in the view capture - and, under them,
a real rule: **a camera's cull mask culls shadow casters from its own view**.
The suit hidden from the eye by layer had no shadow from the eye; the rover
hull likewise from the cab, which is what Mac saw. Hiding by
`SHADOWS_ONLY` instead keeps the shadow (0.20% of the eye's frame for the
suit, 29.6% of a cab frame pitched at the rover's own). Both rigs changed,
the "Suit" layer removed, layer 3 kept as the marker of what is exterior.

**The real ground** came down from NASA over HTTP through the new
`tools/lola-window.py` - 25.6 km at 20 m around the pole, then two 4.1 km
patches at 5 m. The pole is rugged: 66% of the wide window is at or under the
rover's 25°, 63% of the pole patch, 85% of the plateau patch, medians 17-22°.
The pole patch is the scene's ground now, origin on a 3° spot 1.3 km from the
pole with Shackleton's rim 600 m off. The macro reads; up close the 5 m data
is a smooth sheet, which is the detail layer's job. The Gaea bake stays in the
repo until nothing references it.

## The regolith, the sky, the light

Mac asked for a realistic regolith and a sky and ambient to match, the first
look work since the freeze. Built on a new capture, `regolith_capture.tscn`,
which shoots seven views around the sun - down, up, across, the feet, the rim,
150 m up, the sky - and prints a luma for each, so the sweep left numbers
beside the frames.

**What changed.** `surface.gdshader` has a `light()` again, carrying
Lommel-Seeliger with backscatter and an opposition surge behind a per-material
`lunar_brdf` toggle; a `detail` group that bends the normal with a cellular
noise map on the ground plane at two scales and varies the brightness at a
third; and a fill that is warm grey at 0.15 instead of blue at 1.4.
`regolith.tres` is grey with the Vesper bake off and two NoiseTexture2Ds
authored on it; `rock.tres` is grey. `sky.gdshader` replaces the procedural
sky: black, the real sun disc with a glare, faint stars. The environment's
ambient is a warm grey colour source at 0.15. `World.sun_energy` is 0.8. The
head lamp is 3.

**What the sweep found.** The first frames came out white foam: 1.2 of sun on
a 0.5 normal strength, blown into the film stack's red halation. The second
came out salmon with a cyan band under the horizon, with the post stack off
too - and that was the Hearth's mast light and the relay's beacon, both
covering the spawn, amplified by a reflectance model that surges toward a
light near the eye. Sun-only frames were grey at once. The head lamp at 8
then whited out the ground ahead; 3 reads as a lamp.

**Measured.** `LIGHT_COLOR` inside `light()` is energy times pi - a plain
`dot(N, L) * ATTENUATION * LIGHT_COLOR` rendered 60% brighter than Godot's
own diffuse, and `/ PI` matched it to three decimals. `return` is rejected in
`light()`. `LIGHT0_SIZE` is the light's angular distance in radians: a disc of
half that radius measured 34 pixels of area at 70 degrees over 900, which is
the 0.53 degree sun; the procedural sky had been drawing it twice the size.

Frames in `previews/2026-09-14/regolith-*`: `before`, `v1` to `v5`, `after`,
`after-lamp`, `after-lights`, `after-lamp3`, and the contact sheets. All
thirty-two tests pass and the project boots clean.

## Wheel tracks

Mac: "shader based rover wheel tracks left behind the rover". Proposed as a
map the wheels paint and the ground reads - a never-cleared SubViewport in a
toroidal window following the rover, three channels (depth, and the heading
doubled), the tread drawn by the shader from the heading so the map can be
coarse - and built on Mac's yes. [[Tracks]] has the design; the frames are
`previews/2026-09-14/tracks-*`.

**Two probes first.** A never-cleared SubViewport keeps every draw, starts
black, and lands two stamps queued into one redraw - so nothing is lost when
two physics ticks fall between frames. And headless, `frame_post_draw` never
fires and every shader global reads back null, the scanner's included, so the
test holds the arithmetic and the capture holds the picture.

**What the first frames found.** The chevrons were keyed to the world origin,
because the map does not store where a track's centre line is; and at range
they were a moire, because a map has no mip chain. Both fixed by giving the
stamp a U-shaped depth across the tyre - a rut, and a stand-in for the
distance from the centre - and fading the tread by `fwidth`. `smooth` is a
reserved word in the shader language, which cost one compile.

**Built:** `track_map.gd` and its node in `test_world.tscn`; the `tracks`
group and `light()` change in `surface.gdshader`; five globals in
`project.godot`; the regolith's track uniforms; "Wheel tracks" in the F1
panel; `test_track_map.tscn` (55 checks), `probe_track_viewport.tscn`,
`track_capture.tscn`.

**And then the memory.** Mac drove out of range: "would be great to somehow
keep these tracks persistent - maybe a rover track would draw a storable
curve ... persistence is more important than precision." Proposed storing
the path and replaying it into the map as the window moves, over ribbon
meshes along the curve; Mac said build it. `TrackTrail` records every stamp
by 8 m cell; the map paints the remembered stamps back into each strip it
wipes, and into the whole window after a jump. The capture moves the rover
600 m away and back: 1,293 stamps painted back, the from-above frame within
0.35% of the original. Save and load exist and nothing calls them.

**Two things measured on the way.** A driven rover ignores a transform
write - it reads back and is undone by the next physics frame, frozen
kinematic or not - while the parked one takes it; the capture gets the
driver out first, and the cause is not known. And `_draw()` does run under
`--headless`: the dummy renderer calls it and drops what it draws.

**Then footprints.** Mac asked what new animations would cost them, and
the answer was nothing if they read the skeleton rather than the clips, so
they do: `Footprints` on the astronaut takes the toe bones' world positions
each tick, rays down for the ground, and stamps a boot on the up-to-down
edge. `stamp()` and the trail took a width for it. The walk double-stamped
a foot every few strides until the speed threshold allowed for the planted
foot's slide; the toe goes 4-7 cm under the floor. 18 prints in 12 m, a
zig-zag beside the wheel tracks in `previews/2026-09-14/footprints-*`.

**Then dust.** "Puffs of dust from under the wheels" - proposed as the
lunar version, which is no puff: grains on clean parabolas at 1.62, no
drag, landing and gone. Mac said do it. `WheelDust` on the rover, one
`GPUParticles3D` per wheel at the contact point aimed back and up, rate off
the tyre's rpm and skid, gravity from `World`, a heightfield under the
rover for the landing and a box round the hull for the fender. Four
captures to get it right: grains born on the ground died at birth; the
chase camera came back with the driver; at 60% of tyre speed the spray
hugged the wheels, at the tyre's speed and 45° it is a rooster tail.
[[Wheel-Dust]]; `previews/2026-09-14/dust-*`. Mac retuned the sky in the
editor - stars sparser and fainter, less glare - and asked for grains that
darken in shadow: `dust_grain.gdshader` lights the billboard as a sphere,
`dust-unshaded-vs-shaded.jpg`.
