# Session 2026-08-31

Cargo grew consequences. Crates now take damage from how they are carried, and
a delivery pad grades and pays for what arrives - which closes the core loop
end to end for the first time.

## Where the session started

Asked what to build next. The vault had crate fragility as `#now` in [[Cargo]],
which was the right instinct scoped as half a feature: damage that is never
scored is a hidden number, and a hidden number changes nobody's driving. A grep
for `deliver|contract|condition|damage|payout` across the whole project
returned one comment and one local variable in a test. Steps 1 and 4 of the
core loop in [[01-Pillars]] did not exist - the Beacon in `test_world.tscn` was
a cylinder and a light that did not know you had arrived.

So the work was scoped as both halves: the input and the readout.

## The design call

Mac approved the carrier-jolt model before implementation. Recorded in
[[Decision-Log]], and it came out of a constraint rather than a preference:

**A stowed crate cannot measure itself.** It is frozen with its collision
switched off, so it can never receive a contact event - and that is precisely
the case that matters, because almost all cargo damage happens while riding a
rack. So the **rack** measures the carrier and passes the jolt down.

Which turned out to be the more honest model anyway. Strapped-down cargo is not
hurt by its own collisions, it is hurt by the vehicle slamming into things, so
the damage a load takes is a direct read on how the [[Rover]] is being driven.

## Changed in code (Claude)

- `JoltMeter` - measures **proper acceleration**, `|dv/dt - g|`, the thing an
  accelerometer bolted to a crate would read. Free fall reads zero, so falling
  is free and only the landing costs. Gravity is passed in rather than read
  from `World`, so the meter is a pure function of its inputs.
- `Crate` gained `condition`, `fragility` and the damage curve. Damage is
  **integrated over time** rather than fired on a threshold crossing: no edge
  detection to get wrong, no double-counting a landing that spans frames.
- `CargoRack` became the cargo's accelerometer. It finds its carrier by walking
  up the tree, so the same script serves the rover (its parent) and the
  astronaut (its grandparent) without either scene knowing it is watched.
- `DeliveryPad` - grades a crate on arrival and pays
  `base_value * condition ^ 1.5`.
- HUD shows the worst condition aboard each rack, a delivery receipt, and a
  running total. Condition words come from one static shared function, so the
  HUD and the receipt cannot grade the same crate differently.
- `test_cargo_damage.tscn`, `probe_carrier_jolt.tscn`,
  `delivery_capture.tscn`, `probe_spawn_settle.tscn`.

Only `crate.tscn` and `hud.tscn` were edited - both scenes Claude last touched.
`rover.tscn`, `astronaut.tscn` and `test_world.tscn` have Mac's edits on them
and were left alone; the pad is a standalone scene and the capture adds it at
runtime.

## Measured, not reasoned

`probe_carrier_jolt.tscn` drives the loaded rover over real terrain and reports
the distribution. Two things came straight out of it that reasoning had got
wrong:

- **The first guess at the damage floor was 8 m/s^2. Driving peaks at 7.44.**
  Seven percent of margin, on placeholder terrain the rover can only manage
  4.7 m/s across. Moved to 12.
- **The first probe reported a parked rover at 7.71 m/s^2**, which looked like
  a genuine suspension jitter that the floor would have to clear. It was a one
  second settle window. With three seconds it reads 3.96 - one gravity, as it
  should. The probe was measuring its own impatience.

Final spread: parked 3.96, cruising 7.44 peak, a 7 m drop 33 at p99, an 8 m
fall on foot 65 at p99. Ordinary driving now costs exactly nothing, and that is
asserted rather than hoped.

## Broke / noticed

- **Everything in `test_world` has always spawned in mid-air and fallen.**
  `height_at()` returns a height in the terrain node's *local* space, and Mac's
  Terrain node carries a 0.5 scale on Y - so `_place_on_ground` was putting the
  rover, the astronaut, the beacon and every crate at twice the ground height.
  Invisible for as long as nothing measured landings; the moment cargo took
  damage, the world pre-scuffed its own crates to ~0.88 before the player
  touched anything. Crates settle within 6 cm now. Recorded in [[Terrain]].
- **The delivery pad shipped as a bare `Area3D` with no solid deck**, so crates
  fell straight through it and rested on the terrain underneath - still
  detected, still paid, still passing every headless test. Only the look-dev
  render showed it. It has a `StaticBody3D` now.
- **`Rover.enter(null)` fails half way through** and leaves the rover parked
  with `_physics_process` off, so a test that does it silently measures a
  stationary vehicle and reports it as a drive. Cost one confused test run.
- Smoothing turned out to be load-bearing rather than cosmetic. Without it the
  same landing costs four times as much at 120 Hz as at 30 Hz, and the
  astronaut and the rover are not comparable instruments at all. Measured 434
  m/s^2 against 181 raw for a similar event; smoothed, 30-120 Hz varies 2.7%.

## The tuning panel (later the same day)

Mac played the cargo build, confirmed it works, and asked for a debug and
tweaking panel on F1 with sliders for every tunable.

**Built by reflection rather than hand-listed.** It reads `get_property_list()`
and generates a control for anything marked `PROPERTY_USAGE_SCRIPT_VARIABLE`,
which is exactly a script's `@export` vars, with `@export_group` headings and
`@export_range` bounds carried through. Around fifty tunables across seven
scripts, and a hand-written panel would have been wrong the first time either
of us added an export. See [[Debug-Panel]] and [[Decision-Log]].

It also carries the *debug* half Mac asked for: a live readout of frame rate,
gravity, rover speed, the jolt the rack is riding through, and the worst crate
condition aboard.

The panel is deliberately not a second source of truth. Nothing it does writes
to a scene or to `project.godot`; "Copy changes" reports only what differs from
the authored values, as a list to read across into the inspector.

## What the panel forced

- **`World`'s constants became `@export var`.** A `const` cannot be written to,
  so gravity could not be tuned at all. The one-place rule is intact -
  `world_constants.gd` is still the only place a planetary number is written -
  but the names went to snake_case, and everything *derived* from a tunable
  became a function rather than a stored value so it cannot go stale. Seven
  call sites, all mechanical. Recorded in [[The-Planet]].
- **Gravity needed a second write.** `project.godot`'s `default_gravity` is read
  when the physics space is created, so setting it at runtime moves nothing.
  Measured three candidates in `probe_runtime_gravity.tscn`:
  `ProjectSettings.set_setting` left a falling body at exactly its old rate,
  while `PhysicsServer3D.area_set_param` on the world's space tracked to within
  3% at both 1.0 and 20.0 m/s^2. `World` now pushes every change itself.

## Broke / noticed (panel)

- **A test scene whose script fails to compile runs forever.** Godot loads the
  scene without the script, nothing calls `quit()`, and the headless process
  sits there - so the documented "non-zero exit on failure" check hangs rather
  than failing. Cost one five-minute timeout before I read the *top* of the
  output instead of the bottom, where a two-line parse error was waiting. This
  is the second time this project has found a way for a failing test to not
  report failure.
- **`--quit-after` is not the fix for that, it is worse.** It forces exit 0 when
  the frame budget runs out, so it turns both a hang and a real failure into a
  pass. It briefly did exactly that here - three suites reported `exit=0` with
  no PASS line, which is what gave it away. Now recorded in `CLAUDE.md` as
  something never to put in a test command.
- **GDScript has no `%g`.** It compiles fine and then raises "unsupported format
  character" at runtime, once per call, so a formatter in a per-frame UI path
  floods the log rather than failing loudly. `String.num(v, 6)` is the
  short-and-exact equivalent - measured against `str()` and `String.num()`
  before choosing.
- **A `SpinBox` snaps its display to its own step**, and no single step divides
  every value cleanly: an authored 32.0 on a 0-60 range read back as 31.998,
  and 0.35 as 0.35002. Reading a value back wrong is worse than losing the
  arrows, so the numeric field is a `LineEdit`.
- **Engine property groups leak into a reflected panel.** `@export_group`
  entries are indistinguishable from the engine's own, so the delivery pad
  inherited `Area3D`'s "Reverb Bus" as a section heading until the pending
  heading got discarded whenever a non-script property followed it.
- `PackedStringArray(...)` is not a constant expression, so it cannot
  initialise a `const`. A typed array literal can.

## The post stack (later still)

Mac imported a film post stack from StarChef - `shaders/post/` and
`postprocessing_effects.tscn` - and reported a shader error in the editor.

**The error was a missing file wearing a misleading message.** All four shaders
open with `#include "res://shaders/include/post_common.gdshaderinc"`, and only
`shaders/post/` had been copied. Godot's preprocessor does not report an
unresolved include; it hands the raw text to the tokenizer, which stops on the
`#` and says `Unknown character #35: '#'` - which reads like the directive is
unsupported rather than like the target is absent. Copied the include from
StarChef, without its `.uid`.

Mac then turned every effect on and asked for the stack to be optimised, with
the one condition that all the settings survive.

**Merged four passes into one.** It arrived as four ColorRects with a
`BackBufferCopy` between each; `film.gdshader`, which came over in the same
import, already existed to replace exactly that. Measured on a frozen scene
with the grain's clock stopped, so two renders of the same frame are
byte-comparable:

| | ms/frame at 1600x900 |
|---|---|
| No post at all | 0.870 |
| Four ColorRects + 3 BackBufferCopy | 1.174 |
| One ColorRect | 0.965 |

The post work itself went from 0.30 ms to 0.10 - a third of the cost - for a
mean per-channel difference of **0.13/255**, against a no-post control of
2.47/255. All 19 settings survived, none renamed.

**No `BackBufferCopy` is needed at all.** Adding one back changes the image not
at all and costs 0.01 ms: a `canvas_item` shader declaring `hint_screen_texture`
with `filter_linear_mipmap` already gets a full mip chain. That was the real
risk in the merge - glow and halation read at LOD 3-6, so without mips they
would have silently collapsed to a plain copy, which is what the probe was
written to catch.

**The 19 post settings are now on the F1 panel too.** Shader uniforms surface on
a `ShaderMaterial` as `shader_parameter/<name>` with `PROPERTY_USAGE_EDITOR` but
*not* `PROPERTY_USAGE_SCRIPT_VARIABLE`, so they take the same explicit-list path
the wheels use - with the names read from the shader's own uniform list, so that
target cannot drift either. Tuning a look is a thing to do while driving.

`film_material.tres` had been carrying StarChef's tuning, which is a different
look; it now carries what Space Stranding was actually running. StarChef's
numbers are in git at 253e9f4.

## Rocks on the terrain (later still)

Mac asked for rocks scattered on the terrain, distance-culled so it does not
murder performance. Approach proposed and approved before building: cells of
MultiMesh, big rocks solid, procedural placeholder meshes behind an export so
authored ones can replace them. See [[Scatter]] and [[Decision-Log]].

**The load-bearing assumption was measured first, and it was wrong.** The
obvious implementation - one MultiMesh for the field with a
`visibility_range_end` on it - does nothing at all. The range is tested against
the *instance's* AABB, and a MultiMesh is one instance holding every transform,
so a whole-patch MultiMesh has a whole-patch AABB and the camera never leaves
it. A 120 m range dropped **exactly zero** of 1,376,256 primitives. Splitting
into 32 m cells dropped 93% of them.

That inverted the design: cells are the mechanism, not an optimisation on top
of one. And they have a floor too - 16 m cells with nothing culling them
measured *worse* than one big MultiMesh, at 348 tiny draw calls.

Final numbers on the real 512 m patch, 3297 rocks, camera at eye height: 42,640
primitives and 97 draw calls at the 48 m / 170 m defaults, against 379,840 and
263 with culling off. About 0.1 ms on a 4080, which is inside the noise -
primitives and draw calls are the honest signal here, not the wall clock.

## Changed in code (rocks)

- `RockScatter` - `@tool` Node3D beside [[Terrain]], never under it: the
  Terrain node's Y scale would squash every rock parented to it. Rolls
  placements from a seed, rejects on world-space slope and a clump mask, and
  buckets survivors into cells of one MultiMesh per variant per detail tier.
- `RockMesh` - noise-displaced icospheres, flat shaded on purpose. The
  painterly shader quantises light into three bands, and a smooth boulder turns
  those into concentric contour rings where facets take one band each.
- `Terrain` gained a `rebuilt` signal, so retuning the ground moves the rocks
  with it instead of leaving them in the air.
- `rock_painterly.tres`, forked from the shader as [[Visual-Direction]] asks.
- `test_rock_scatter.tscn`, `probe_scatter_cull.tscn`,
  `probe_scatter_cost.tscn`, `probe_multimesh_readback.tscn`,
  `scatter_capture.tscn`.

No scene files were edited. The scatter is not yet in `test_world.tscn` - that
file carries Mac's edits, and the capture adds the node at runtime instead.

## Broke / noticed (rocks)

- **A MultiMesh's per-instance transforms do not survive `--headless`.** The
  dummy renderer accepts `set_instance_transform()` with no error and hands
  identity back from `get_instance_transform()`. The new test asserted on those
  transforms and confidently reported every rock 12 m off the ground - a
  failure that was entirely the null driver. The same writes round-trip exactly
  under the real renderer. `instance_count`, `custom_aabb` and
  `visibility_range_end` do survive; measured both ways in
  `probe_multimesh_readback.tscn`.

  That is the third time this project has found a way for a test to report
  something other than the truth, and the first where it *failed* wrongly
  rather than passing wrongly. The fix is that the scatter now keeps its own
  record of what it placed - which is also the only way anything else in the
  game will ever be able to ask where the rocks are.

- **Rocks were being buried by 70% of their own height.** `sink` was a fraction
  of the rock's *diameter*, but boulders are squashed on Y, so a half-height is
  barely a third of a diameter. Every headless assertion passed - they were on
  the ground, just mostly in it - and the field read as flat plates lying on
  sand. Only a render showed it. `sink` is now measured against the mesh's own
  AABB.

- **A probe that errors mid-frame reads exactly like a hang.** A diagnostic
  with its `quit()` after the code that failed never reached it, and every
  later frame early-returned, so the process sat there forever. Same family as
  the compile-error hang already recorded, different cause. Piping through
  `tail` made it worse by swallowing the error until EOF that never came.

- The first pass rendered rocks darker than the regolith they sit on, so they
  read as holes in the dust rather than as stone.

## The rover camera stopped rolling over (later still)

Mac asked for the rover camera's rotation to be clamped: it inherited the
chassis basis whole, so an upside-down rover meant an upside-down player. The
lean itself was wanted - it is what makes a side slope read as a side slope -
so this clamps rather than levels. `tilt_limit_deg` is 18.

Measured through the rover's own camera with the chassis posed: 15 degrees of
roll tilts the camera 15, and 45, 90 and 180 all tilt it 18.

**Rebuilt from scratch each frame rather than counter-rotated.** A correction
written back into the same local basis it was read from compounds - it looks
correct on frame one and then winds the camera round over a few seconds, which
reads as drift rather than as a broken clamp. The pivot's orientation is
assembled from three independent parts instead: a clamped up vector, the
chassis heading projected into that plane so the camera still sits behind the
rover through a turn, and the player's yaw on top.

That is what forced the player's yaw out of the pivot's rotation and into a
`float` - the basis is overwritten every frame, so it cannot also be where the
yaw is stored. `StickLook` grew a `read()` returning the stick's deltas;
`apply()` is now a wrapper around it, so the astronaut is untouched.

`tilt_smoothing` (0.12 s, a time constant rather than a per-frame factor) is
there because the rig passes suspension chatter straight through and the clamp
would otherwise snap on and off against it. 0 restores the old rigid feel.

`test_camera_levelling.tscn` asserts the clamp, and also the two ways this can
look right and be wrong: a camera pinned flat to horizontal passes every clamp
assertion and is a different feature, and a camera that has eaten the heading
no longer sits behind the rover. It checks 120 frames at a fixed attitude for
drift.

**One failure was mine, not the code's.** The drift assertion read the pivot's
tilt straight after posing the chassis, but the pivot is a *child* - re-posing
the body drags its global basis along, so the baseline was the previous test's
leftovers. The 18.00 it "drifted" to was the correct answer all along.

## The spring arm, and the sliders that were there but nameless

Mac asked for the upside-down spring-arm collapse fixed and the camera controls
put on F1.

**Two separate faults in the arm, and the obvious one was not the expensive
one.** The mount was left in body space, so it followed exactly the roll the
levelling exists to ignore: upside down it sat below the chassis and the arm
swept into the ground, collapsing to 2.21 m of 9. It now hangs off the
*levelled* basis. Separately, `SpringArm3D` excludes nothing by default, so the
rover was shoving its own camera - measured at **1.09 m of 9** whenever the view
pitched up, which swings the arm down through the engine bay. That is not the
wreck case at all, that is looking up while driving, and it had been there the
whole time.

Both fixes were checked by breaking them again afterwards. Worth doing: the
first assertion written covered the mount but *passed* with the exclusion
removed, so it would have shipped an untested fix. The chassis case needed its
own test at a pitched camera.

**The F1 camera controls were already there and unlabelled.** `@export_subgroup`
arrives in `get_property_list()` as its own entry flagged
`PROPERTY_USAGE_SUBGROUP`, which is neither a group nor a script variable - so
it fell through the discard that keeps engine headings out, and the heading
vanished. The three levelling knobs rendered as anonymous rows under "Camera".
Silent in exactly the way a reflected panel always fails.

Handling subgroups also surfaced the engine's own on the wheel target:
"Suspension" and "Damping" had been in the list and unshown since the panel was
written. 101 tunable rows now.

I had told Mac the levelling knobs were "on F1 under Camera → Levelling" in the
previous message. Half true - they were on F1, but there was no Levelling
heading to find them under, because nothing rendered one.

## Orders: from an inbox note to a working slice

Mac dropped `00-Inbox/order management.md` and asked to brainstorm on it. Three
forks in it were real, all three got called, and then the slice got built.

### The forks, settled

- **An order is a named crate**, not a requirement filled from stock. The note
  implied both; the UI flow said "requirement" and the definition said "crate".
  The named-crate reading fits what exists, and the other one is what Facility
  Storage grows into if hauling ever feels thin.
- **The terminal panel moves cargo Storage → Dock and no further.** A menu that
  assigned crates straight to the rover would have deleted the physical stow
  verb and the derived centre of mass from 2026-08-30.
- **Deadlines are parked, not rejected.** A clock says the opposite of what
  `payout = condition^1.5` says. The conditions under which the two coexist are
  written down, along with the [[Flares]] interaction that blocks it: sheltering
  from a flare must not cost you the window.

Then Mac overruled the one recommendation Claude pushed back on. Handing an
order back **reconditions the cargo**, on the grounds that a run which went
badly should be restartable. The objection - that it launders damage - is
weaker than it looked, because recall returns crates to the *origin*: it is a
repair priced at a round trip, and it closes a worse hole, since an order can
never be made unfinishable by a box at the bottom of a ravine.

### What that costs, and where it is logged

The price of reconditioning is exactly the drive back. So **facility spacing now
sets the cargo economy as well as the pacing**, and that was already the
`#blocking` question in [[Settlements-and-Cast]]. The two facilities in
`test_world` sit 66 m apart, which is far too close for the price to be real.
Logged as a `#playtest` in [[Orders]].

### Three bugs the design could not have found

All the same shape - correct and unusable:

- **A docked crate is stowed, so `E` slides straight past it**, and `F` only
  knew about the rover. The board issued cargo nothing could pick up, and every
  headless assertion passed because the crates were exactly on their slots.
- **Loose cargo could never be delivered.** The pad only takes crates whose
  order is accepted, and found cargo is never on a board to accept from.
  Picking it up now accepts it, which is also the better fiction.
- **`recall()` would have freed an authored crate**, deleting one of Mac's
  placed nodes with nothing to put it back.

None would have surfaced from a headless test written off the design. They
needed *can a player actually do this*, which is what the capture scene is for -
and the first capture also showed the facility was three props scattered on open
sand until they shared an apron.

### Measured, not guessed

`probe_facility_sites.gd` scans the terrain for the flattest 10 × 4 m footprint.
The first two facilities were placed by eye at `y = 0` and were underground; the
sites they moved to have 0.72 m and 0.56 m of spread across the whole footprint.

The StarChef TSV editor came over **unchanged** and read `orders.tsv` on the
first try - byte-identical round trip through its own `load()`/`serialize()`,
columns typed, 63-line preamble preserved. Only the bash launcher needed a
PowerShell counterpart. That is the second time the generic design has paid for
itself without being asked to.

## Aim-based interaction

Mac played the order slice, hauled cargo, fulfilled orders - it works - and came
back with the thing that was in the way: a crate lying beside the rover is
always picked up, because `E` tried crates first. Mac asked for an interaction
*cone* instead of the sphere.

**Built as scoring rather than as a cone**, which is a better fit for the same
intent. Godot has no cone collision primitive, and a `ConvexPolygonShape3D` one
would have to be re-oriented every frame to follow the camera. So the sphere
stays as the broad phase and everything it finds is scored on how well it lines
up. The priority list is gone from both verbs.

Worth recording that this **finishes** the 2026-08-30 two-verb decision rather
than reopening it. That split `E` from `F` so boarding could never compete with
unloading; what it left was an ordering *inside* `E`, and that ordering is what
Mac hit.

### Measured

`probe_interact_aim.tscn` sweeps the look direction through a full circle and
reports which arc gives which answer. The first version of the probe asked a
malformed question - it aimed at the crate every time, so the crate always won
and every row read "never". Fixed to hold the geometry still and sweep the look:

| | crate | rover | neither |
|---|---|---|---|
| crate beside the rover | 109° | 83° | 168° |
| crate almost in the way | 160° | 20° | 180° |
| crate well off to one side | 113° | 104° | 143° |
| crate on the far side | 160° | 160° | 40° |

The 20° row is kept rather than tuned away. With the crate nearly on the
sightline to the rover, looking at the rover *is* looking through the crate;
fixing it would need `aim_bias` near 12 against the 3.0 that serves everything
else, and a step to either side resolves it anyway.

### The tests got better by being forced to aim

Every press now has to be aimed first, so `test_cargo_flow` and `test_orders`
both needed work. That is an improvement: they assert what a player experiences
rather than what the code happens to accept. `test_orders` had been passing only
because the dock happened to sit on the astronaut's default look axis.

`test_interact_aim.tscn` is new and covers the bug in both directions, checks
that turning your back reaches nothing - without which a scorer ignoring the
angle entirely would still pass - and checks the HUD prompt tracks the aim. The
prompt and the verb are now literally the same call rather than two that agree.

## Storage as a real place

Agreed as the next piece. Two calls went to Mac up front and both came back as
the recommendation: **uncapped**, and **accepting an order still spawns onto the
dock** rather than routing through storage.

### Records, not nodes

A warehouse of frozen `RigidBody3D`s is a lot of physics for cargo nobody can
see or touch, so a deposit records what a crate *is* and frees the node. The
interesting part is where that leaves [[Cargo]]'s founding rule - a crate is the
same node its whole life - and the answer is that **the rule is about being
carried**. A crate still cannot launder damage by riding on a rack.
`test_storage.tscn` asserts condition survives the shelf, because a warehouse
that quietly repaired things would undo the whole abandonment argument by the
back door.

### Two old wrongs it let us fix

Both had been wrong since the day they shipped, and neither had anywhere better
to go until there was a shelf:

- **Dock overflow was dumped on the sand** - loose bodies beside the pallet, and
  a way to lose cargo under the terrain.
- **A delivered crate sat on the pad forever**, so a depot silted up with cargo
  already paid for. Consumed now, not shelved: fifty spent crates would bury the
  player's own things under a receipt log.

`recall()` also had to learn to sweep the shelf, or an order whose cargo had
overflowed into storage could be taken a second time while the first lot sat in
the warehouse.

### The sign

Mac added hand-placed `Label3D`s to see facility names from a distance. Folded
into `facility.tscn` and driven from `display_name`, so the text cannot drift
from the facility it names. `probe_sign_size.gd` measured it rather than
guessing: `fixed_size` with `pixel_size` 0.0004 reads the same at 62 m and at
12 m, which is what a navigation label wants. Mac's `DebugLabels` node is gone -
it is in git at 6276cfc if the hand-placed version is ever wanted back.

### Two self-inflicted delays worth remembering

- A `&&` chain short-circuited on a failed assertion, so one of three patch
  scripts silently never ran. Everything compiled, because the half that was
  missing was the half nothing referenced yet. **Check that a script that should
  have printed actually printed.**
- Two test hangs, both the same cause: iterating an untyped `const Array` yields
  `Variant`, which makes `:=` fail to compile *two lines later* and points at the
  wrong variable. A test scene whose script will not compile runs forever, so
  this reads as a hang rather than as an error. Now in `CLAUDE.md`.
