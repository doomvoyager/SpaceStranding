## Art direction (later the same day)

Mac brought four of their own space paintings and asked for a stylized

covered palette, materials and the fixed star but never *how* things are drawn.

- Mac chose **saturated** over the doc's muted palette, and **surface treatment
  only** (no post pass). Both recorded in [[Decision-Log]];
  [[Visual-Direction]] rewritten to match, including what it changes about
  modelling.
- Built `res://shaders/painterly.gdshader` - banded diffuse with a custom
  `light()`, hue-shifted shadow, no specular, fresnel rim, triplanar brush
  stamps. Presets in `res://materials/`. `terrain.gd` gained an exported

  scene had to be touched.
- Added `res://tests/look_dev_capture.tscn`, which boots the real world and
  writes stills from fixed vantage points to `user://look_dev/`. Must run
  windowed - `--headless` is the dummy renderer and produces no image.

## Broke / noticed (art pass)

Three failures, all found by rendering rather than by reasoning:

- `AMBIENT_LIGHT` does not exist in `fragment()` or `light()` on 4.7.1. Probed
  both. Shader now disables engine ambient and carries its own fill.
- **Engine ambient erased the banding entirely** - terrain rendered as an
  untouched smooth CG gradient while the flat-faced test boxes banded fine,
  which is what gave it away.
- **Hard bands turned the soft-shadow penumbra into a loud ordered stipple.**
  Chased it to volumetric fog first (wrong - disabling it changed nothing), then
  to the real cause: folding `ATTENUATION` into the value being quantised
  thresholds Godot's per-pixel PCSS filter noise. Both this and the
  `AMBIENT_LIGHT` fact are now in `CLAUDE.md`.

Also noticed: brush stamps at a fixed world size alias into moire at any
distance. Fixed with a power-of-two distance ladder that keeps cell size
roughly constant on screen while staying world-locked, so they should not swim.
**Should** - nobody has driven it yet.

## Cargo and controller (later still)

Mac asked for a simple cargo pickup system and for the gamepad to go in early,
while there is little enough input code that parity is cheap.

- Answered the `#blocking` question in [[Cargo]]: **slot grid, not physics
  placement**, with the load feeding back into the rover's mass and centre of
  mass. Both calls Mac's, both in [[Decision-Log]].
- Built `CargoRack` - one script, six slots on the rover's roof in a 2×3 grid,
  two on the astronaut's back. **The slots are Node3D children**, so capacity
  and layout are authored by dragging markers and no slot count exists in code.
  Occupancy is derived by walking the slots, never cached.
- A crate is a `RigidBody3D` that stays the same node its whole life; stowing
  freezes it, kills its collision and reparents it into a slot. Nothing is
  destroyed and respawned, so a crate cannot lose its identity - or, later, its
  damage - by being carried.
- Rover `mass` in the inspector is now the *empty* rover. A full rack is +22%
  mass and lifts the centre of mass from −0.35 to −0.10, spending exactly the
  roll margin the low centre of mass was there to provide.
- **Two verbs, not one priority-ordered interact.** `E`/`A` deals with the
  world, `F`/`X` moves cargo both directions. A single context key hands you a
  crate when you walk up to a loaded rover meaning to drive it.
- Full gamepad parity, plus `StickLook` shared by both camera rigs - stick look
  has to be polled because a stick holds a position where a mouse emits deltas.
- HUD: controls card and two context prompts. The HUD asks the astronaut what
  each key *would* do rather than re-deriving the rules, so a prompt cannot
  drift from the behaviour.
- Added `res://tests/test_cargo_flow.tscn` (round trip + load maths, drives the
  rover 11 m and asserts the crate is still exactly on its slot) and
  `res://tests/cargo_capture.tscn` (stills of the loaded racks).

## Broke / noticed (cargo pass)

- **`test_rover_controls.gd` had always exited 1**, printing PASS and reporting
  failure, because `get_tree().quit(0)` only *schedules* the exit and execution
  fell through to `quit(1)`. The documented "non-zero exit on failure" check had
  never worked. Fixed in both tests, and confirmed by sabotaging the rack and
  watching the cargo test go red.
- **Chased a phantom cyclic dependency.** A plain headless boot reported every
  new `class_name` as undefined - including `StickLook`, which depends on
  nothing. `Crate` and `CargoRack` name each other's types and looked exactly
  like the culprit, so a flag went in to break the "cycle". Wrong: the global
  class cache is only rebuilt by `--import`. Measured it afterwards, found the
  mutual reference compiles fine, reverted the flag and deleted the false claim
  from `CLAUDE.md` before it could mislead anyone.
- Stowed crates sit on collision layer 0, so the camera spring arm passes
  through the tower on the astronaut's back instead of being shoved into their
  helmet. Not planned; load-bearing now, and recorded in [[Cargo]].
- Godot renamed nothing quietly: the rover already had a node called
  `CargoRack` (the deck mesh). Renamed it `RackDeck` rather than let two
  siblings collide.
- Verified in stills, not by reasoning: 6/6 fits the deck without intersecting,
  and the two-high back stack reads as intended. It also fills a good deal of
  the third-person camera's lower frame - authentic to the parent, possibly bad
  to play. Flagged `#playtest` for Mac rather than changed.

## Pedals (immediately after)

Mac looked at the gamepad map and moved throttle off the stick: **`RT`
accelerates, `LT` decelerates, stick steers only.**

- The stick could not just be unbound - `move_forward` / `move_back` are shared
  with the astronaut on foot. The rover got its own `drive_forward` /
  `drive_back` (`W`/`S`, `RT`/`LT`, no stick binding), so the same stick means
  different things in the two contexts without either script inspecting the
  input's source.
- `LT` brakes above 0.6 m/s forward and reverses below it. Brake-only would have
  left a gamepad with no reverse at all now that the stick is throttle-free.
  `S` changed to match; the two should not diverge.
- `test_rover_controls.tscn` grew from two checks to five, and now asserts the
  thing Mac actually asked for: holding `move_forward` leaves the rover coasting
  *down* (17.9 → 13.0 m/s) rather than accelerating. Both regression tests had
  been driving the rover with `move_forward` and needed updating.

## Analog scaling (Mac on the pad)

Mac played it and reported two things: inputs behaving like switches, and `RT`
locking the steering. Both real, and only one of them was where it looked.

- **Walking was genuinely binary.** `_apply_horizontal_movement` normalised the
  wish direction, throwing away the magnitude `get_vector()` had already put
  there. Half a stick walked at full speed. Keeping the throw separately fixes
  it; the keyboard produces exactly 1 and is unaffected, which is why it had
  never shown up.
- **The trigger was already analog** - `probe_analog_input.gd` synthesises real
  joypad events through the real `InputMap` and shows RT at raw 0.5 giving 0.444
  strength, and no leakage at all from either trigger into the steering actions.
  So the steering lock was not an input-map problem.
- **It was the steering falloff ramping from 0 m/s.** Authority eroded from the
  first metre per second: 32° at rest, 26.9° at walking pace, 14.9° by 11.6 m/s.
  Since the only way to gain speed is the throttle, that reads exactly as "RT
  locks the steering". Added `steer_falloff_start` (5 m/s) so the ramp begins
  above manoeuvring speed. Measured before and after with
  `probe_steer_under_throttle.gd`.
- `test_analog_input.tscn` locks in all three: half stick walks at 1.20 m/s of a
  3.20 m/s walk, half trigger gives 55% of full acceleration, and full lock
  survives the throttle at manoeuvring speed.

Worth noting for next time: deadzone **rescales**, it does not just gate. Raw
0.5 through a 0.2 deadzone is 0.375. That is why "half a stick" is not 50%.
