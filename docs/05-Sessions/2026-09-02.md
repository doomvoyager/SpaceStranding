# Session 2026-09-02

Mac asked what was next, then set the order: make 0.55 g permanent, then build
the haulable mast. Both done. The mast took two commits because the survey and
the verb are separate ideas and the survey is the one that makes siting a
decision.

Also committed at the top of the session: the whole of 2026-09-01's second and
third halves, which had never been committed - the spawn gizmos, `SpawnPoint`,
the `TuningWriter` and [[Placement]].

## Changed in the editor (Mac)

- Nothing this session. One scene edit was asked for and granted: `Mast105` in
  `test_world.tscn` now carries `deploys_as`.

## Changed in code (Claude)

- **The planet is 0.55 g.** `surface_gravity` 3.34 -> **5.39 m/s^2**, mirrored
  into `project.godot`. Fifteen prose references to "0.34 g" followed it.
- **`max_engine_force` 900 -> 1170**, re-seated by bisection. See below.
- **`Lattice.survey_at(x, z)`** - the coverage question asked of ground that is
  not a site yet, answering with a new `SiteSurvey`. Scores every registered
  site and keeps the sturdiest, where sturdy is the *weakest* of two margins:
  link range left over, and the smallest gap between the sight line and the
  ground. Same rule `probe_relay_site` used to choose the world's own relay.
- **`Lattice.line_clearance()`**, kept separate from `has_line_of_sight()`
  because that one early-exits on the first blocked sample and this one has to
  see them all.
- **`Crate.deploys_as`** - what a crate becomes when raised. A property rather
  than a subclass, so a new deployable opts in by declaring what it becomes.
- **`Crate.raise_into()` and `Relay.lower()`** - the crate becomes the structure
  and is stowed inside it rather than consumed, so lowering hands back the same
  node with its damage intact.
- **`raise_mast` on `R` / gamepad `Y`**, with `KIND_MAST` wired into the
  astronaut's aim-scored target system and excluded from the other two verbs.
- **A carried survey readout in the HUD**, refreshed four times a second.
- New: `test_mast_survey`, `test_mast_readout`, `test_mast_raise`. Eighteen
  tests now, all green.

## Broke / noticed

- **0.55 g broke the rover, and no test noticed.** All fifteen regression tests
  passed at the new gravity with the old engine force, while ten seconds of full
  throttle over broken ground fell from **4.7 m/s and 29 m to 1.9 m/s and 7 m** -
  a hauler that can no longer haul. Grip scales with weight so the corners
  improved, but rolling resistance and climbing out of undulations scale with
  weight too, and on broken ground they win. Only `probe_carrier_jolt` saw it,
  because it is the only thing in the project that drives. Re-seated to 1170 by
  bisection against the old figures: same 29 m, load still pristine. 1450 covers
  37 m and starts scuffing cargo on an ordinary drive.
- **Falls now cost about twice as much.** `jolt_floor` and `jolt_ruin` are
  absolute m/s^2 and were deliberately left alone - a heavier planet making a
  drop more expensive is the physics working. A 7 m drop of the loaded rover
  went from costing a tenth of the load's condition to costing 0.22.
- **Every raised mast was going to be called "relay".** The code named one only
  if its id was blank, and `relay.tscn` carries an authored id - so the check
  never fired, and the second mast raised would have silently replaced the first
  in `_sites`, leaving a mast standing in the world that coverage could not see.
  It shipped green. What caught it was raising a *second* mast, which the test
  did not do; it does now. One is never enough to catch a name collision.
- **`is_stowed()` only knew about racks.** A crate riding inside a raised mast
  reported itself loose and went on sampling its own jolt from inside something
  static. Harmless today - it reads one gravity, well under the damage floor -
  and wrong in a way that would have surfaced later as mystery cargo damage.
- **Two commits appeared mid-session that Claude did not make** (`e74f538`,
  `ff5b9c7`), 22 seconds apart, sweeping up staged content and splitting it
  along the placement/tuning line with messages nobody here wrote. No git hooks
  in the repo. Content verified correct and complete against what was staged,
  and no gravity work leaked in. Flagged to Mac; unexplained.
- The four-times-a-second survey interval is a guess that has never been felt
  with a pad in hand.

## Broke / noticed

- **The map mesh's winding was inverted, and it did not look like a winding
  bug.** Wrong winding does not draw nothing: it draws the faces pointing
  *away*, so a heightfield seen from above renders as a few bright slivers on
  the steepest far slopes and everything else as void. That reads as a colour
  or lighting problem, and it cost a long chase through the elevation ramp,
  auto-ranging and a gamma curve — all three of which were real improvements
  and none of which was the bug. What named it was rendering the mesh one flat
  colour and **measuring** the result: 0.1% of the viewport drawn. New verified
  engine fact, and the winding is now copied from `terrain.gd`.
- **The map drew no coverage at all.** It and `CoverageMap` both hang off
  `Lattice.coverage_changed` and both rebuild deferred, so whichever ran first
  won — and when the map won it read a mask that did not exist yet, set
  `coverage_enabled` false and never looked again. It listens to the mask's own
  `rebuilt` now. A dependency on a *thing* should not be expressed as a
  dependency on the event that also causes it.
- **Two of the four capture framings were looking at empty space.** The terrain
  node carries a 1.3 km offset, so a camera framed on the world origin spends
  most of the frame off the patch — which read as "the map is black" and sent
  the colour chase off in the first place.
- **Showing terrain outside coverage contradicts a pillar.** Raised before
  building, Mac chose it, recorded in [[Decision-Log]] rather than left as a
  silent contradiction.
- Markers pile into unreadable noise when zoomed out. Open in [[The-Map]].

Mac had added cabin blockout geometry to `rover.tscn` between sessions and

## The brake light

Emissive `StandardMaterial3D` on the bar; `rover.gd` drives the emission
energy. Colour is authored in the inspector, behaviour is in the script,
neither has an opinion about the other, and the F1 panel gets the brightness
for free because the rover was already a discovered target.

**It follows the pedals, not `brake`.** That is the whole design. Engine
braking applies 2.5 brake units whenever the throttle is closed, so a light
wired to the brake *force* is lit almost permanently - and a brake light that
is always on is indistinguishable, in a screenshot, from one that works. Only
the full brake and the decelerate pedal count. Reverse counts too, so the bar
does not blink off at the instant the rover comes to rest under the pedal.

**Energy 0.9, arrived at by looking.** The first attempt was 5.0 and rendered
an **orange-white smear** that glowed over the whole hull - ACES desaturates
hardest where the image is brightest, which is the same trap the
[[Visual-Direction]] overlays hit. Swept against the real scene at twilight,
the brightest ambient this planet ever has: 0.5 reads as paint, **0.9** is red
with just enough bloom to read as lit, 1.4 starts washing, 5.0 is gone.

`test_brake_light.tscn` asserts the material's emission energy - not just the
boolean - through throttle, coast, brake, decelerate, reverse and exit. Checked
by wiring the light to `brake > 0` and confirming the coasting assertion fires
and the process exits 1.

## Rollover recovery

Two design calls put to Mac before anything was built.

**Who rights it: the astronaut, from outside.** Not the driver from the seat,
which was the cheaper build. Climb out, walk round, hold the key - it is a job
you get out and do, and you are standing in the weather for the length of it.

**What it costs: time, and nothing else.** A flip already cost you the crash;
the recovery does not bill you twice.

**On `interact`, held.** This looks like it reopens the mast decision and does
not: that rejected ambiguity between two *different objects*, and this is one
object in two states. An upside-down rover cannot be boarded.

**Driven, not thrown.** An angular impulse on a 950 kg body in low gravity has
to be large, depends on how the thing is lying, and does whatever it does to
the load by accident. The body goes kinematic and its transform is
interpolated by hand over 2.4 s onto a landing height solved from a ray and
the wheels' own radius.

The cargo claim is measured, not asserted: six crates on the roof, peak jolt
**9.14 m/s^2** against a `jolt_floor` of 12, **zero** condition lost. And the
assertion was proved load-bearing by breaking it - at 0.25 s instead of 2.4 the
same recovery peaks at **30.96** and starts taking the load apart.

### Two things that went wrong, both worth keeping

**The test billed the crash to the recovery.** It captured the load's condition
*before* flipping the rover, so the first run reported 0.048 lost and failed a
mechanism that was working. Landing a loaded rover on its roof costs real
condition and that is the crash being paid for. The baseline moved to after the
wreck settles.

**`ground_below()` returned `Variant` and took the whole project down.** A
`-> Variant` used to mean "or null" poisons `:=` at every call site, and the
parse error names the *caller's* variable with nothing pointing at the
function. Same family as the untyped `const Array`. Fixed by taking the
fallback as a parameter.

### What it exposed

**You could not reliably climb out of a wreck to reach the verb.** `exit()`
handed the exit marker's *body-space* position straight to `disembark()`, so it
followed every degree of roll the rover had: upside down, half a metre up on
the wrong side; on its flank, 2.1 m straight down and inside the terrain.
Harmless for as long as nothing needed you outside a wrecked rover, and
load-bearing the moment something did. Now the offset is taken in a levelled
frame and the height solved against the ground - the author-the-XZ,
solve-the-height rule the crates already had and nothing else had been given.

## The signs are a scan result now

`scripts/world/site_sign.gd`, on the `Sign` node of `facility.tscn` and
`relay.tscn`. Dark until `Q`, then it lights on the pulse's own envelope with
the site's name and how far off it is, and goes out with the tags on the same
frame. `pixel_size` 0.00018, down from 0.0004.

**It replaces the scanner's facility tag rather than sitting beside it.** Mac's
call from three options. `Scanner.tag_groups` has stopped listing `facility` and
`relay`, because with both, a site inside the pulse's reach was named twice -
once at the mast and once seven metres below at ground level. The sign carries
the distance now, which is what the ground-level tag was for. Terminal, dock,
storage and pad are still tagged and are the reading that matters once you have
arrived.

`sign_range` is 200 m against a 70 m pulse, so the wave never physically
reaches a distant sign. It lights when the front hits its own limit instead,
which reads as the return coming back rather than as a switch.

**The size was rendered, not chosen.** `probe_sign_size.tscn` was re-run
downward - 0.00012 through 0.0004 at 200 m and at 30 m. 0.0004 dwarfs the
building it names; 0.00012 loses its strokes to the outline against red
terrain. 0.00018 sits just under the scanner's own `tag_size` of 0.00022, which
is the right relationship: the sign is further away than anything the scanner
tags.

`Scanner._viewer_position()` is now public `viewer_position()`. The sign
measures its distance from there rather than keeping a second copy of the
rover-or-foot rule, which is the bug `Astronaut.vantage()` exists to have only
once.

## The speedometer

Bottom right, on screen only while driving. **m/s, Mac's call** - km/h reads
more like a vehicle instrument and gives a livelier 0-23, but m/s is the unit
every other number in this project speaks, so what you read while driving is
what you tune with.

`Rover.ground_speed()` is horizontal and signed off `forward_speed()`. Not
`linear_velocity.length()`, which would make a rover dropped off a ledge read
*faster* the further it falls.

Set in JetBrains Mono, which was already in the repo and used nowhere. A
proportional font shifts the digits sideways as the number changes, which is
what a readout updating sixty times a second must not do. One theme override on
one label; it comes straight back out if Mac would rather it matched the
prompts.

itself to catch up now. This is the `_process` / `_physics_process` trap again,
in a new place: the readout is not stale *state*, it is a correct drawing of an

# Signs that stand out of each other's way

Mac, on the note above: fix it.

## How

**The arbitration lives on the scanner, and the sign asks for it.**
`Scanner.sign_has_room()` decides once a frame which names fit, nearest-to-the-
ping first, refusing anything within `sign_separation_px` (200 px) of a name
already placed. It is the tag declutter's rule with its own number, because a
site name plus a distance is a much wider label than `Dock`.

A manager node was the obvious alternative and is worse: the signs are children
of `facility.tscn` and `relay.tscn` and have to stay draggable in the editor,
and the scanner is already the thing that knows where the ping came from, what
the envelope is, and which labels are on screen. One instrument, one arbiter.

**The sign asks rather than being told.** A flag pushed from the scanner is a
frame stale by the time the sign draws, and the sign spends that frame visible
in a slot it has already lost.

**Ordered by distance from the ping, not from the player**, so a name cannot
swap places with its neighbour while you drive past. Same anchor the tag reveal
already uses.

New tags are also refused a spot on top of a sign. A tag *already placed* is
not moved - tags are sticky once created, and a site further off than something
taggable beside it lights its sign after that tag has claimed its spot. They end
up on adjacent lines rather than through each other, which is legible, so it
stands rather than being chased with tag eviction.

## Broke / noticed

**One frame of a name drawn in a slot it had already lost.** The signs run
their own clocks in their own `_process`, in tree order, and a sign crosses from
"the wave has not reached me" to "I want to draw" *during* that pass - so the
sign that ran first triggered the resolve while a later one still looked idle,
leaving it absent from the answer it was about to consult. Absent read as "no
reason to refuse you". A sign missing from a frame's answer now forces a fresh
one. The crowding test caught it on the first run, which is the only reason it
is not still in there: one frame of one label is not something you see.

**`unproject_position()` works fine under `--headless`.** `test_scanner.gd` had
switched its tag separation off with a comment saying screen-space declutter
"needs a real projection to mean anything" - a guess, made once, and inherited
as fact. It is arithmetic on the camera against `get_visible_rect()`, which the
dummy display server reports at the configured 1600x900; a point dead ahead
lands on the centre pixel. Measured by `probe_headless_unproject.tscn`, written
because the neighbouring fact about synthesised mouse events never reaching the
GUI makes this exactly the sort of thing that would not work. So the declutter
is regression-tested rather than left to the captures.

**`sign_slot_count()` lied when the arbitration was off.** `_resolve_signs()`
bailed early with an empty dictionary, which is *correct* for behaviour - a
missing entry reads as "has room" - but made the diagnostic report nought of
nought while two names were on screen. Caught by reading the test's own output
rather than its exit code.
