### Findings from the proposal round, for the work queued next

- **The Moon alone will make the rover faster, not slower.** Nothing caps speed
  today, and the last gravity change went the other way (29 m -> 7 m in 10 s).
- **Mass cannot be tuned live**: `refresh_load()` rewrites it from a value
  captured at `_ready`. And in `VehicleBody3D`, suspension and grip scale with
  mass, so mass alone only makes the rover sluggish.
- From Godot's source, **to be probed**: `max_engine_force` applies per driven
  wheel (1170 is 7,020 N), and the brake is a per-wheel, per-tick impulse.
- **Horizon arithmetic**: 2.43 km from a 1.7 m eye, but the ground drops only
  1.7 m at 2.4 km and 22 m at a 3x3 corner against 210 m of relief - so it culls
  small things, not hills, and vacuum has no fog to hide the world's edge.
- **At a sixth of the gravity, bumps behave the same at ~0.55x today's speed**,
  and braking distances roughly triple. "Tips before it slides" is not a lunar
  fact: gravity cancels out of it, and `wheel_friction_slip` decides it.
- **No recorded decision rules out first person.** The cab box is about 0.77 m
  deck to roof, too low for a seated eye - Mac's blockout call.

### Broke / noticed

- **A reading of Godot's source was wrong, and a probe said so before any code
  was built on it.** The theory: `VehicleBody3D` never caps straight-line force
  by grip, so a traction model was needed on top. A slip of 0.2 braked at 0.65
  m/s^2 whatever the brake - it does cap it, at about 2 x slip x g. What it
  does *not* do is match that sideways (about 0.65 x slip x g), which is why the
  grip was chosen for steering and the forces for everything else.
- **Two readings of the source were right**: `engine_force` goes whole to every
  driven wheel (6.25 m/s^2 against 7.39 per wheel), and `brake` is per tick (8.7
  against 12.3 m/s^2 at 60 and 120 Hz).
- **Moving to 1.62 with the old tuning left 31 of 32 tests green** while the
  inside wheels lifted in every corner and the rover still did 8 m/s on the
  flat. The one failure was not the rover at all.
- **That failure was air.** Godot's default linear damping of 0.1/s capped a
  fall at 16 m/s, and the speedometer test's 30 m drop landed just under the
  8 m/s it waits for. Zeroed in `project.godot`; the test passes.
- **1200 N was sluggish, not careful**: progress halved on an 18° slope and ten
  seconds over broken ground covered 15 m. Drive force was then chosen on the
  climb - 2000 brings the scanner's red back to 25° and covers 26 m.
- **The probe's own mistakes, both caught by its numbers**: a sag formula that
  added the rest length (a constant 25 cm off at both gravities, with the
  gravity difference right), and a "slide" of 18.5° at walking pace that was
  the body's geometry in a turn, not a slide.
- **Physics runs in real time under `--headless`.** A governed rover never quite
  reaches its cap, so "reach 4 m/s" waited out a minute per attempt and the run
  was killed at 300 s. `--fixed-fps 60` runs the same probe in a second with
  byte-identical output. (Also: zsh does not word-split an unquoted `$ARGS`, so
  one sweep quietly applied only its first override.)
- **The empty rover rolled away.** Nothing set the brake until somebody climbed
  out, and on lunar grip it crept 4.2 m in ten seconds. Setting it in `_ready`
  did nothing at first - `get_physics_process_delta_time()` is 0 there - until
  `brake_impulse()` fell back to the tick rate. Now 0.07 m. Also a new fact.
- **The Moon reads purple.** Black sky, white sun and a hard edge are right; the
  terrain is the pink colour bake under the materials' blue fill, and crates and
  hulls have blue shadow sides. Look work - left for Mac, with
  `previews/2026-09-13/moon-before-*` and `moon-after-*` side by side.
- **At full lock the loaded rover neither tips nor slides**: the front washes
  out, and the circle widens from 7 m at 2 m/s to 10 m at the cap.
